01 — About

About Me

I am a Research Fellow (Post-Doctoral) in the Department of Architecture and Civil Engineering at Toyohashi University of Technology (TUT), Japan. My work sits at the intersection of urban systems, transport networks, and computational modelling — asking how cities can be made smarter, safer, and more livable in the face of digitalization, population aging, and climate risk.

My doctoral research investigated how digital services substitute or complement physical travel demand, producing two major outcomes: a modified urban microsimulation model incorporating digital accessibility and social network dynamics; and the QOL/QOB accessibility evaluation framework applied comparatively to Aichi, Singapore, and Munich — adopted by the e-ASIA consortium as a shared assessment tool across Japan, the Philippines, and Thailand.

Earlier, I served as Head of the Transportation Planning Department at Kabul Municipality, leading large-scale urban transport projects for a city of over 4 million people in collaboration with the World Bank (KUTEI Project) and JICA.

02 — Research

Research Themes

My research integrates computational modelling, accessibility theory, and urban policy analysis — asking how cities evolve under digitalization, aging, and climate risk, and what tools planners and policymakers need to respond.

Theme 01
🏙

Air-Front Smart City Evaluation

Developing QOL and QOB accessibility indices to evaluate Smart City proposals integrating physical and cyber spaces. Applied comparatively to Aichi, Singapore, and Munich as part of the JST e-ASIA project.

QOL/QOBSmart CitiesAccessibilitye-ASIA
Theme 02
🌐

Digital Service Substitution

Quantifying how ICT-enabled digital services replace or complement physical travel demand, reshaping social networks and urban structure in aging Japanese society. Foundation of a nationwide questionnaire study.

ICTTravel DemandSocial NetworksJapan
Theme 03
🔁

Urban Microsimulation

Developing and validating a multi-layer microsimulation model incorporating digital accessibility, social network dynamics, and population aging — calibrated on actual statistical data from Japanese cities.

Agent-BasedLUTIMulti-LayerAging
Theme 04
🚨

Disaster Resilience & Evacuation

Modelling evacuation choice behaviour under compound disaster risks (natural hazards + COVID-19 pandemic), and water scarcity spatial accessibility analysis in large-scale post-earthquake scenarios in Japan.
